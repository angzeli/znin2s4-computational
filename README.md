# znin2s4-computational

Computational workflows and analysis supporting the study of precursor-derived ZnIn2S4 thin-film architectures for photocatalysis.

## Overview

This repository is the computational component of an Imperial College London final-year chemistry project. It investigates how crystal structure, surface chemistry, charge management, and interfaces influence photocatalytic reduction chemistry in ZnIn2S4 thin films.

The intended device architecture is NiOx / ZnIn2S4 / Co-Pi. The computational programme compares pristine ZnIn2S4, NiOx/ZnIn2S4, ZnIn2S4/Co-Pi, and NiOx/ZnIn2S4/Co-Pi to determine how buried-interface and reaction-facing modifications affect charge separation and surface reactivity.

## Research Questions

- How do ZnIn2S4 polymorphs and polytypes change structural stability and electronic properties?
- How strongly do surface and facet choices affect adsorption and reaction chemistry?
- Can H2O2 formation through two-electron oxygen reduction serve as a preliminary probe of reduction-side photogenerated-electron utilisation?
- Which early steps control CO2 activation and reduction on ZnIn2S4 surfaces?
- Does a buried NiOx interface improve hole management without introducing detrimental trap states?
- Does reaction-facing Co-Pi improve hole management while preserving access to reduction sites?
- Are bottom-interface and top-surface engineering complementary, redundant, or competitive?

## Computational Scope

The planned scope includes bulk ZnIn2S4 structure benchmarking, surface models, adsorption and reaction intermediates, NiOx/ZnIn2S4 interfaces, Co-Pi/ZnIn2S4 models, and charge-density and electronic-structure analysis. Supporting molecular calculations may be added where they provide a useful complement to periodic models.

The current periodic electronic-structure work uses VASP. Structure generation, crystallographic validation, and post-processing may additionally use tools such as Python, spglib, pymatgen, ASE, Bader analysis, LOBSTER, and VASPsol; molecular calculations may use ORCA when introduced. Mention of these tools describes the intended project scope and does not imply that every workflow has been completed.

## Current Work

The implemented work is currently concentrated in WP1, the ZnIn2S4 polymorph/polytype benchmark. The starting structure set contains:

- experimental cubic spinel ZnIn2S4;
- ordered alpha1 derived from the experimentally disordered IIIa parent;
- reconstructed beta derived from experimental IIb;
- legacy experimental IIa as the starting structure for an unconstrained IIa to IIa-prime relaxation;
- experimental IIb.

The repository includes the source CIFs, reconstructed structures, the alpha1 ordering script, structure-provenance documentation, and PBE-D3(BJ) bulk geometry-optimisation inputs and retained outputs for the five WP1 structures. Later surface, reaction, and interface work described in the roadmap remains planned unless corresponding files are added.

## Computational Methodology

The current WP1 bulk inputs use VASP with PBE, D3(BJ) dispersion correction, PAW datasets, a 500 eV plane-wave cutoff, and full optimisation of atomic positions and lattice vectors. The electronic and ionic convergence targets are 1 x 10^-6 eV and 0.01 eV/angstrom, respectively, with structure-dependent Gamma-centred k-point meshes.

These settings document the present WP1 calculations rather than a universal prescription for future surface, interface, magnetic, charged, or molecular models. Method-specific convergence and validation are required as the project expands.

## Reproducibility

- Experimental source structures retain their database and literature provenance.
- Reconstructed structures are distinguished from directly experimental structures and are generated by scripts where available.
- Curated structures, computational inputs, scripts, and scientifically useful outputs of manageable size are version controlled.
- Licensed VASP `POTCAR` files and large regenerable runtime artefacts are excluded from Git.
